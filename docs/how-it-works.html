Hearth Companion

A home companion for an older person living alone — it sees, talks, runs her errands within limits her family set, and asks her before it ever alarms anyone.
The idea: bounded agency. AI agents are about to start acting — and spending — for people. Hearth acts freely within limits, stops and asks a relative above them, and brings a human in only when it matters. Elder care is the case; the pattern is general.

What it does — three flows

FLOW 1 · MONEY

Groceries over budget

"Order my groceries for the week."

Hearth builds her usual basket (€84). It's over the €60 weekly limit, so it stops and asks: Claire gets the basket and the excess on Telegram with Approve / Reject. One tap approves exactly that purchase, once, for 15 minutes → Stripe charge → Hearth tells Marie, with the delivery time. A second tap charges nothing.

FLOW 2 · SIGHT

It can see her

"Can you see me? Do I have my glasses on?"

The camera's latest frame and a one-line scene description travel with every question, so Hearth answers from what it actually sees. It only talks about the room when asked — never narrates on its own, never identifies people.

FLOW 3 · FALL

Asks first, then family

"Marie? I noticed you're on the floor. Are you okay?"

Two consecutive "on the floor" readings (~4 s) → a heads-up to Claire → Hearth speaks first. "I'm fine" closes it. Silence or "I'm not okay… yes" → Telegram alert with a photo of the room and an "I'm on it" button. A false alarm costs one spoken question.

How it works

What happens when she falls — the timeline

t = 0 s camera sees her on the floor (sequence of frames, not a still — a fall, not sitting down) t ≈ 4 s second confirmation → incident opened · Claire gets "⚠️ Possible fall — checking with Marie now" t ≈ 5 s Hearth speaks first: "Marie? I noticed you're on the floor. Are you okay?" t ≈ 5–25 s listens "I'm fine, I dropped my glasses" → resolved · Claire gets "✅ False alarm" silence, or "I'm not okay / help / yes" → alert with room photo + [I'm on it] later Claire taps I'm on it → Hearth: "Claire has seen it and is on her way."

The rules — written in code, not left to the AI

  • The camera never alarms directly. Two confirmations to ask; escalate only on silence, a request for help, or an explicit "yes".
  • The vulnerable person is never the sole approver of a large spend. Anything over her weekly limit goes to a relative.
  • One approval, one charge. An approval is consumed in a single atomic database statement; a second tap, an expired approval, or a changed basket charges nothing. Payments are idempotent.
  • Budget read from the database every time — a limit changed on the dashboard applies immediately.
  • Emergency services are never called automatically. A human decides.
  • Sight is answered, not narrated. It never claims it can't see, and never identifies anyone — it describes what's visible.
  • Privacy. Frames are analysed for an event and not stored; only the frame attached to an alert leaves the house — to the family.
  • Designed for a real impaired user: no blinking lights, nothing to press, repeated questions answered as kindly the fortieth time as the first.
